Cours Julien sits on a hill in the very centre of Marseille, a ten-minute walk from the Vieux-Port. The easiest way is the metro, which stops at the foot of the neighbourhood. But the curious will arrive by the steps.

In short

  • Metro: line 2, Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien station.
  • From Saint-Charles station: line 2 direct, two stops.
  • Tram: line T3, Rue de Rome, then a five-to-ten-minute walk uphill.
  • Bus: lines 54, 74 and 81.
  • On foot: about ten minutes from the Vieux-Port.
01The easiest way

By metro,
line 2

The station is called Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien, on line 2 of the Marseille metro. When you come out, you’re in the heart of the neighbourhood: Cours Julien, Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont and the great staircase are two minutes away.

From Saint-Charles station, it’s direct: take line 2 towards Sainte-Marguerite Dromel. Notre-Dame-du-Mont is the second stop, just after Noailles. From the airport, the shuttle bus goes to Saint-Charles station; from there it’s just those two metro stops.

Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien station · IngolfBLN, CC BY-SA 2.0

02For strong legs

The tram,
then the steps

Tram line T3 runs the whole length of Rue de Rome, right at the bottom of the neighbourhood. Get off at one of its stops between Noailles and Castellane, then walk up. Allow five to ten minutes, and a few steps.

It is the best way to arrive. Via Cours Lieutaud and the Rue Armand-Bédarride steps, built around 1864 and covered in graffiti, or via the streets climbing up to Cours Julien, you discover the neighbourhood the way it was built: on a slope, in steps, in painted walls.

04The centre on foot

On foot

From the Vieux-Port, allow about ten minutes: walk up the Canebière, then turn right towards Noailles and the streets climbing to Cours Julien. From Noailles, it’s five minutes. From Castellane, just under a quarter of an hour via Rue de Rome.

The neighbourhood itself is best explored on foot. The streets are narrow, often pedestrianised, and full of steps. Wear good shoes.

Cours Lieutaud, at the foot of the neighbourhood · Alexey Komarov, CC BY-SA 4.0

05If you must

Car, bike

By car, we don’t recommend it: the streets are narrow, often one-way, and parking is a feat, especially in the evening. The easiest option is to leave the car in a city-centre car park or a park-and-ride, then finish by metro.

By bike, it’s possible, but the neighbourhood sits on a hill: you’ll earn the climb. An e-bike helps a lot.

06Hotels and hostels

Where to stay

You don’t need to sleep at the Cours Ju to enjoy it. The neighbourhood has few hotels, but it is a few minutes away on foot or by metro from Noailles, the Old Port, Le Panier and Saint-Charles station. The Mama Shelter, rue de la Loubière, is the closest.

Charming hotels, youth hostels, tips for choosing an area: our addresses, with photos and links to the official websites, are in the Mag.

Frequently asked questions
Which metro station for Cours Julien?

Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien station, on line 2 of the Marseille metro.

How do I get to Cours Julien from Saint-Charles station?

Take metro line 2 at Saint-Charles, towards Sainte-Marguerite Dromel: Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien is the second stop, after Noailles. Allow about ten minutes.

Can I walk to Cours Julien from the Vieux-Port?

Yes, in about ten minutes, walking up the Canebière and then the neighbourhood’s streets. The last stretch is a bit of a climb.

Where to stay near Cours Julien?

The Mama Shelter is a few minutes’ walk away. Otherwise, the hotels and youth hostels of Noailles, the Old Port, Le Panier and Saint-Charles are 10 to 20 minutes away on foot or by metro. Our picks are in the article Where to stay near the Cours Ju.
