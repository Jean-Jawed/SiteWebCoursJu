In Marseille, the walls speak loudly, and nowhere more than in Cours Julien. Since the 1980s, the neighbourhood has been a canvas for generations of graffiti artists. The works don’t last: they get painted over, answer each other, disappear. That is what makes it the city’s finest open-air gallery.

The walk at a glance

  1. The great Cours Julien staircase, covered in graffiti, next to the metro.
  2. Cours Julien: shutters, façades, fountain.
  3. Rue Pastoret and Rue des Trois-Rois, the most colourful streets in the neighbourhood.
  4. Rue Vian and the Bédarride steps, on the way down.
  5. Invader’s mosaics, to hunt for all over the city.

About 1 hr 30 on foot · Free · Metro Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien

01The starting point

The Cours Julien
staircase

This is the picture everyone comes for. The great staircase links Cours Julien to the streets at the bottom of the neighbourhood, and not a centimetre of bare stone is left: steps, railings, walls, lamp posts, everything is painted. The layers have been building up for years, so the staircase never looks the same twice.

It is a stage, too. In 2014, the artist Mahn Kloix painted a monumental anamorphosis on it, an image that can only be read from a single viewpoint. The steps have been repainted in rainbow colours, have hosted photo exhibitions laid straight on the stone, and serve as seating on summer evenings.

02The esplanade

Cours Julien,
the neighbourhood’s shop window

At the top of the staircase is the esplanade. All along Cours Julien, every metal shutter is an artwork and every façade has been painted over at least once. Marseille’s tourist office lists a piece by Lapin Thur, made in 2015, among the neighbourhood’s landmark works.

A tip: the metal shutters can only be seen when the shops are closed. Early in the morning, on Sundays or Mondays, the esplanade shows a different exhibition from the one you see in the afternoon.

03The beating heart

Rue Pastoret,
Rue des Trois-Rois

This is where the density is highest. On Rue Pastoret, murals cover the façades from top to bottom: huge black-and-white portraits, lettering, comic-book characters. Shop windows are painted right up to the signs.

Then wander the nearby streets at random: Rue des Trois-Rois and its smiling cats, Place Paul-Cézanne, Rue Poggioli, Rue Bussy-l’Indien, Rue Crudère. You’ll spot the tags of Gamo, Difuz or Joke, and dozens of anonymous artists.

Rue Pastoret · Fred Romero, CC BY 2.0

04On the way down

Rue Vian
and the Bédarride steps

Pedestrianised Rue Vian is lined with vintage shops, designer boutiques and painted walls. It is the street for wandering, where motorbikes park in front of the murals and shopkeepers have let artists decorate their shopfronts.

To head back down to Cours Lieutaud, take the Rue Armand-Bédarride steps. They were built around 1864, when Cours Lieutaud was cut through the hill as a trench. Their walls, too, are covered in graffiti.

05Mosaics

Invader,
the invasion of Marseille

Invader, the French artist who has been sticking mosaics inspired by the video game Space Invaders on cities around the world since the late 1990s, “invaded” Marseille in summer 2020. For his exhibition Invader Was Here, at MAMO, on the roof of Le Corbusier’s Cité Radieuse, he put up more than 80 mosaics, from Les Goudes to L’Estaque.

Marseille has become the most invaded city in France after Paris. Some pieces are tiny, others several metres wide. They have Marseille motifs: a seagull, a heart on a street sign in Le Panier. To find them, the Flash Invaders app lets you photograph them and score points. Keep your eyes open on the way up to Cours Julien: there are Invaders hiding in the neighbourhood too.

The seagull · The heart on Rue Beauregard, in Le Panier · Mike is Michi, CC BY-SA 4.0

06Before you go

Our tips

When

Morning or Sunday

The light is softer, the painted shutters are down and the streets are quiet.

With a guide

Guided tours

On certain days, Marseille’s tourist office runs a tour of Cours Julien’s street art with a specialist.

Respect

People live behind these walls

Behind the façades are homes and shops. Ask before photographing people, and don’t paint without permission.

Afterwards

A drink, a gig

The walk ends a stone’s throw from the neighbourhood’s terraces and music venues.

Frequently asked questions
Where can I see street art in Marseille?

The Cours Julien neighbourhood (Notre-Dame-du-Mont, Marseille 6th) is home to most of Marseille’s street art: the great Cours Julien staircase, Rue Pastoret, Rue des Trois-Rois, Place Paul-Cézanne, Rue Crudère and Rue Vian.

Where is the Cours Julien staircase?

The great staircase links Cours Julien to the streets at the bottom of the neighbourhood, a short walk from Notre-Dame-du-Mont – Cours Julien metro station (line 2). Its steps and walls are entirely covered in graffiti.

Where can I find Invader mosaics in Marseille?

Invader put up more than 80 mosaics in Marseille in summer 2020, from Les Goudes to L’Estaque, notably in Le Panier, around the Vieux-Port and near the Cité Radieuse. The Flash Invaders app lets you find them and “flash” them.

Can I take a guided street art tour in Cours Julien?

Yes, on certain days Marseille’s tourist office runs guided tours of Cours Julien’s street art. You can also follow the walk described in this article on your own: about 1 hr 30 on foot.
