MarchThe wildest

The carnival
of La Plaine

It has no sponsors and no official floats. The independent carnival of La Plaine, Noailles and Les Réformés has been run by local residents since the late 1990s. Everyone makes their own costume, brass bands improvise, and the procession winds through the streets around Place Jean-Jaurès.

The high point is the Caramantran. This giant effigy, which embodies the ills of the past year, is put on trial in public, paraded through the neighbourhood, then burned on the square. The 27th edition took place on Sunday 29 March 2026. Don’t confuse it with the city’s official carnival, which parades around the Vieux-Port in April.

In 2019, for its 20th anniversary, some forty photographers gathered two decades of carnival pictures in a book, Carna Oaï. It is the best way to understand what the festival means to the neighbourhood.

MayFree

La Plaine
du Rock

One Sunday in May, Place Jean-Jaurès turns into a festival. The Phocéa Rocks association, which has championed Marseille’s independent rock scene since 2012, and the venue L’Intermédiaire set up several stages there, named in 2025 after the neighbouring streets: Trois-Mages, Chave, Ferrari, Saint-Savournin.

On the bill: rock in every form, from punk, garage and post-punk to metal and pop. La Plaine du Rock took over from the Rue du Rock, held on Rue Consolat from 2013 to 2019. The first edition was in May 2024; the third, on Sunday 31 May 2026, brought together sixteen bands on four stages, for free.

21 JuneEverywhere

Fête
de la Musique

On 21 June, Marseille’s tourist office says it itself: Cours Julien is the epicentre of the festival. Every bar brings out its speakers, bands play on the terraces, DJ sets run late into the night, and the crowd drifts from stage to stage between Cours Julien, Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont and La Plaine.

Local traders often publish a map of the concerts: in 2023, it listed 23 stages. On La Plaine, a free rock, punk and pop line-up fills the afternoon. A tip: come early, and on foot.

SeptemberBack to school

Fête
du Cours Ju

Back after the summer, the town hall of the 6th and 8th arrondissements holds a festival weekend on Cours Julien, built around creation, music and sharing. In 2026, on 19 and 20 September: graffiti workshops and performances, listening sessions, a concert on Saturday night, then a shared meal with music and a salsa show on Sunday.

OctoberAt Le Traquenard

The Miss Cagole
contest

Forget Miss France. On La Plaine, the only crown that counts is won at Le Traquenard, the bar on Place Jean-Jaurès, which has held the Miss Cagole contest since the 1990s, back when it was still called L’Univers. The contest stopped for around twenty years before coming back in 2018.

The cagole is the Marseille woman in all her glory: free, flamboyant, outspoken, loud and unapologetic. Contestants face a jury “as serious as it is wild” in four rounds: singing, dancing, banter and look. The prize? A jar of mustard, and the most honorary of titles.

The latest contest took place on Saturday 17 October 2026, on the terrace of Le Traquenard, with L’Intermédiaire, La Plaine de Fadas and the La Plaine traders’ association. That evening, protests were taking place all over France, and the organisers chose to keep the event going. For them, Miss Cagole has always been “a political manifesto”: celebrating strong women who own who they are is also a way of protesting.

Being a cagole isn’t a coat of nail varnish. It’s the claw underneath.

Not to be confused with Miss Cagole Nomade, a separate contest open to everyone, held every December at the Espace Julien. The next one is announced for 11 December 2026.

DecemberMakers

Christmas
markets

Since the late 1990s, the Marquage association has run the Grand Marché de Noël des créateurs every December, a makers’ Christmas market on Cours Julien and Place Jean-Jaurès. Around a hundred stallholders from all over France: ceramics, jewellery, textiles, illustration, handmade objects. In 2025, it ran from 6 to 21 December, 10am to 7pm.

On a smaller scale, La Mûrisserie, the community space at 44 Cours Julien, also holds its own Christmas market in mid-December, with makers, workshops and music.

Frequently asked questions
When is the La Plaine carnival?

In spring, usually at the end of March, on a Sunday. It sets off from Place Jean-Jaurès and ends with the trial and burning of the Caramantran. The 27th edition took place on 29 March 2026.

What is the Miss Cagole contest?

A contest held at Le Traquenard bar, on La Plaine, since the 1990s. Contestants compete in singing, dancing, banter and look; the winner takes home a jar of mustard. The latest contest took place on 17 October 2026.

What’s on in Cours Julien for the Fête de la Musique?

On 21 June, Cours Julien is one of the hubs of the festival in Marseille: concerts in bars and on the squares, DJ sets, a rock stage on La Plaine. Local traders often publish a map of the stages.

Is there a Christmas market in Cours Julien?

Yes, the Grand Marché de Noël des créateurs, run by the Marquage association, sets up on Cours Julien and Place Jean-Jaurès every December, with around a hundred stallholders.
