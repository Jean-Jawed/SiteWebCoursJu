Cours Julien, La Plaine and around, Marseille 6e. Everything is within a quarter of an hour’s walk.

On a Friday night in Cours Julien, music comes from everywhere: a basement window, a half-open door, a terrace where someone has brought out a guitar. The neighbourhood has more live music venues than any other corner of Marseille, and almost all of them are within a quarter of an hour’s walk.

To find your way around, we linked them with a single cable, from the smallest stage to the largest. It is not a ranking: every format has its own pleasures. At the start, there are forty of you and the band plays two metres away. At the end, there are 990 of you in front of the stage. In between: the beer bar, the pub, the cellar, the club. It is up to you where to plug in.

01The tiny roomAround forty people

Les 9 salopards

Community venue · gigs · exhibitions · records

We start up close, on rue Crudère, a small pedestrian street a stone’s throw from rue Vian. Les 9 salopards is a community-run venue: a cultural centre that mixes exhibitions, gigs and the sale of records, books and crafts, with the aim of showcasing Marseille’s art scene.

Around forty people, and the band plays two metres away: it does not get any smaller, and that is the whole point. It is one of the neighbourhood’s essential live music addresses, where local bands and alternative scenes play, often for just a few euros.

02The oddballTiny room, low ceiling

Le Gouffre

Far-out gigs · surprise line-ups

At 32 rue Estelle, the Gouffre (“the abyss”) lives up to its name: a low-ceilinged room where people squeeze in for far-out gigs, the kind you will not see anywhere else. The programming is random, the bands often unknown, and that is exactly what people come for.

A word of warning, though: you never quite know whether the place still exists. Before going, check the @zbeul_corp Instagram account. If the door is open, you will have a story to tell.

We cross La Plaine to rue Saint-Pierre: a small venue hidden inside a musicians’ rehearsal space.

03The rehearsal spaceSmall capacity

Leda Atomica

Rock · unclassifiable · homemade

The name comes from a Dalí painting, and it hides one of Marseille’s oldest rock bands. Leda Atomica was formed in the 1980s around Phil Spectrum, then grew into a collective with its own label, LAM. In 2001, the gang opened a workspace for all kinds of music on rue Saint-Pierre, a stone’s throw from La Plaine.

That is what makes the place special: here, musicians host other musicians, and their audience, in the space where they rehearse every day. Rehearsal rooms, a recording studio, a small gig room: the nights are often unusual, and that is exactly why people go.

Back on rue Crudère, a few doors from Les 9 salopards: a beer bar that turned its back room into a stage.

04The beer barAround 70 people

La Voie maltée

Beer · gigs · stand-up

On rue Crudère, behind a graffiti-covered front, La Voie maltée is first and foremost a beer bar: draughts, bottles, sharing boards and barrels for standing tables. But at the back of the room, two speakers frame a small stage.

For a few years now, the bar has run one of the most regular gig programmes in the neighbourhood. Around 70 people, a bar atmosphere where you always end up listening to the band rather than the conversation. And once a month, the stage switches to stand-up.

Just round the corner, on rue Vian: a stage, a pool table and distorted guitars.

05The pubAround a hundred people

La Maison hantée

Blues · rock’n’roll · metal

On rue Vian: a pink front, two carved masks and a wooden door that looks as if it creaks. La Maison hantée (“the haunted house”) has been here for more than thirty years, and describes itself as a family home “haunted by its memories”. Inside: a pool table, table football, paintings on the walls and generous food inspired by the owner’s mother’s cooking.

Musically, it is a rock and metal pub: gigs range from blues to “seriously angry” metal, by way of rock’n’roll. Around a hundred people, a small stage, a small terrace. The kind of place where you come in for a beer and stay for the band.

Off to place Jean-Jaurès, and down into the basement room.

06The cellarIntimate room

L’Intermédiaire

Rock · ska · reggae · chanson

On place Jean-Jaurès, L’Intermédiaire looks every bit the neighbourhood bistro: colourful decor, craft beers, matches on screen. But the real action is downstairs, in a low-ceilinged room set aside for gigs, one of the best in Marseille for alternative bands.

The programming refuses to pick a side: rock, chanson, reggae, ska, Occitan music, electronic music, DJ sets. After a few months of work and a change of owner, the place reopened on the same principle: gigs almost every night, with the band within arm’s reach.

07The brewpub340 m² of event space

La Brasserie communale

Microbrewery · gigs · theatre

Antoine, César and Loïc come from the Équitable Café, a Marseille venue known for its cultural programme. With the Brasserie communale, by the fountain on cours Julien, they brought everything together: a bar, a restaurant, an exhibition space and a microbrewery, visible behind a glass wall at the back of the room.

The heart of the place, for our purposes, is the 340 m² given over to events: gigs, performances, theatre and screenings, with a stage, sound and video. And once the gig is over, the house beer does not have far to travel. The trio later opened a cocktail bar, La Soifferie, which we visited on our cocktail tour.

Now for proper concert venues, with box offices and soundchecks, starting on place Paul-Cézanne.

08The venueAround 150 m²

Le Molotov

Reggae · rock · soul · electronic

On place Paul-Cézanne, the former Balthazar became the Molotov in December 2012. Around 150 m², a real stage, a box office and a programme that runs from reggae to electronic music. For many people in Marseille, it is the neighbourhood venue, where they saw their first gig without their parents.

The Molotov nearly disappeared: in 2016, the prefecture considered closing it down over noise complaints. A petition gathered more than 5,000 signatures in under twenty-four hours. The venue is still here, and has even opened a little sister, the Dodu, in La Belle de Mai.

A step outside the neighbourhood, on rue Ferrari: 270 places and four nights a week.

09The club270 places

Le Makeda

Rap · electronic · rock · parties

For years, 103 rue Ferrari was called the Poste à Galène. In 2019, a new team led by Francine Ouedraogo Bonnot renamed it the Makeda, after the Queen of Sheba. The venue aims to be friendly, community-minded and committed, with 270 places and four nights a week.

The programming knows no borders: rap, electronic, rock, and nights with a loyal following. Gâter le coin, whose takings go to an association helping unaccompanied foreign minors; Rasputin, a queer and Italo-disco collective; and the Saturday Boum, where everyone sings along to 1990s and 2000s hits.

10The springboard280 places

Le Café Julien

Emerging artists · gigs · DJ sets

This is the newest address on the list, or almost. The Café Julien, the Espace Julien’s small venue, reopened on 2 October 2026 after several months of work. By merging with the building’s main hall, its capacity has gone from 180 to 280. A large window now opens it onto cours Julien, the acoustics have been redone, and the bar is wheelchair accessible.

The idea is to be “the missing link between intimate stages and big venues”: the place where a band that has filled L’Intermédiaire can take the next step. And on Thursdays, from 10 am to 5 pm, it opens during the day: exhibitions, workshops, local radio broadcasting live.

Same address, next door: the big hall.

11The big hall990 places

L’Espace Julien

Contemporary music · headliners · local scene

For thirty years, the Espace Julien has kept the cours beating to the rhythm of live music. It is the neighbourhood’s big hall: 990 standing, 600 seated, artists from all over the world and Marseille bands playing their biggest show. And yet the venue insists: “Here, artists and audience are close together.”

Since 2023, the venue has been run by La Responsabilité des Rêves, a cooperative of four Marseille cultural venues, including… the Makeda, three stages back. In the neighbourhood, things come full circle: small stages help bands grow, big ones welcome them, and everyone meets at the bar afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Where to see live music in Cours Julien?

The neighbourhood has around a dozen venues: community-run spaces (Les 9 salopards, Le Gouffre, Leda Atomica), bars with a stage (La Voie maltée, La Maison hantée, L’Intermédiaire, La Brasserie communale) and proper concert halls (Le Molotov, Le Makeda, the Café Julien and the Espace Julien).

What is the biggest concert venue in Cours Julien?

The Espace Julien, at 39 cours Julien: 990 standing, 600 seated. The Café Julien, in the same building, has held 280 people since it reopened in October 2026.

Where can I hear rock or metal in Cours Julien?

La Maison hantée, 10 rue Vian, has been a rock and metal pub for more than thirty years: gigs from blues to metal, pool and table football. L’Intermédiaire, on place Jean-Jaurès, also programmes a lot of alternative rock.

Where can I see a gig in a small venue in Cours Julien?

Les 9 salopards, 2 rue Crudère, holds around forty people. Right next door, La Voie maltée, a beer bar at 7 rue Crudère, runs regular gigs for around 70 people. La Maison hantée, on rue Vian, holds about a hundred.

Has the Café Julien reopened?

Yes, on 2 October 2026, after several months of work. Its capacity has gone from 180 to 280, a large window opens it onto cours Julien, and it welcomes visitors during the day on Thursdays.
