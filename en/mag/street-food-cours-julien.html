In Cours Julien, people eat on the move. Between two bars, before a gig, at three in the afternoon because lunch got skipped: the neighbourhood always has somewhere open, a counter, a window where something is coming out of the fryer.

Over the past year, snacking here has gone up a gear. On Rue Pastoret, within fifty metres, you go from Vietnam to Apulia. The empanadas come from Buenos Aires, the durums from the Levant, the kafteji from Tunis, the royal sandwich from Pakistan, the mechada from Chile. To make sense of it all, we’ve put everything in a vending machine, sorted by shape: folded, rolled, cut in half, for sharing. Eighteen slots, nearly all of them cheap. Each time you try one, stamp your ticket: the snack quest begins.

A1A little piece of ArgentinaArgentina

Rincón Argentino

Empanadas · bar · Argentinian grocery

You have to look for it, on Rue des Bergers, a little street running down from Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont. Behind the window, a little piece of Argentina tucked away: Esteban and Alejandra, from Buenos Aires, shape their empanadas by hand, porteño style. Beef, chicken, ham and cheese, spinach, humita, fugazzeta: you order three, then one more.

It’s snacking done right: quick, cheap and excellent. You eat under the Maradona posters with a glass of Malbec, and you’re not the only fans: Balerdi, Rulli and Medina used to eat here before leaving OM this summer.

A2The Apulian turnoverApulia

Panpan Zerotti

Panzerotti · brand new · terrace

At 28 Rue Pastoret, the Apulian panzerotto has found its Marseille twist. A fried turnover, golden and crisp, that gives way to properly good melted cheese: indulgent, and brand new.

Inside: beef daube, aubergine parmigiana, polpette… You eat it walking, or on the yellow chairs of the street terrace. Open Tuesday to Saturday, noon to 11pm non-stop: perfect for a savoury afternoon snack.

A3The filled doughnutSweet and savoury

Bibou Beignets

Doughnuts · sweet · savoury

A few doors down, right next to Le Chat Perché bar, a little shop with a painted front. The doughnuts are fried and filled in front of you, and there are dozens of them: pistachio cream, salted butter caramel, or savoury Italian-style with mozzarella, pesto or ricotta.

It’s indulgent, and the team are lovely: you dither for ages, they help you choose, you leave with two. It’s the only stop on the quest where you can start with dessert.

A4The challengeMexico

Un Mexicain à Marseille

Quesadillas · empanadas · nachos

The row’s bonus is on Place Paul-Cézanne, a stone’s throw from Le Molotov. Empanadas, giant quesadillas oozing cheese, nachos, tacos, burritos: the full menu of a Mexican snack bar, with halal options.

A warning: come properly hungry. The portions are gigantic, and that’s the whole point. There are a few seats on the terrace, but most orders are taken away.

B1The flatbread everyone’s talking aboutLevant

Durum

Durum · home-made lavash · brand new

On Rue Saint-Michel, in a former launderette opposite Les 2 Libanais, this is the second spot from Sofiane Benouamane, a chef recognised by Gault & Millau. Brand new, and already the talk of the neighbourhood: his flatbreads are exceptional.

The lavash is made in-house and rolled around braised meat or grilled vegetables, somewhere between Turkey, Lebanon and Armenia. Mezze to share round out the menu, but it’s the durum you come back for.

B2A dream come truePakistan

Le Paki

Pakistani · street food · open late

Pakistani food, street food style, on Place Jean-Jaurès: we’d dreamed of it for ages, and it’s become a real treat. The star is the royal sandwich, wrapped in naans. Heavenly.

Around it: tandoori, biryani, home-made curries, dahl, cheese or garlic naans, to eat in or take away. And it’s open late, every day: the end-of-the-night stop.

B3The orange corridorMexico

Tacos Tacos Tacos

Tacos · wine · brand new

On Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont, a narrow corridor in warm colours: orange walls, a long counter, stools. You want to lose yourself in it. The menu is written up on the slates.

It’s brand new, and it’s not just about the tacos: the wine is good too. Grab a seat at the counter, a glass, two tacos, and the evening has begun.

B4Spoilt for choiceMexico

Le Petit Diable

Taqueria · home-made tortillas · brand new

On Rue Crudère, where Wok and Rolls used to be, a taqueria opened at the end of September. It’s brand new too, and you’re spoilt for choice: a taco trio that changes often, always a veggie option, home-made sauces.

The tortillas are made on site from nixtamalised corn. Somewhere between street food and a family canteen, open evenings Tuesday to Saturday, and lunchtimes Thursday to Saturday.

C1The corridor counterVietnam

Bánh Mì 24

Bánh mì · Vietnamese

Back on Rue Pastoret, at number 7. A counter as narrow as a corridor, and often a queue on the pavement: here, everything revolves around the Vietnamese sandwich. Warm baguette, crispy pork, Viet mayo, pickled veg and coriander, in four recipes to customise.

On the side: spring rolls, mango sticky rice and Vietnamese coffee. With Panpan Zerotti and Bibou Beignets, Rue Pastoret on its own becomes a round-the-world trip in fifty metres.

C2For a quiet mealMorocco

Kazdal

Moroccan · home-made · halal

On Rue des Trois-Mages, a Moroccan sandwich shop: classic, traditional, home-made. Generously filled breads, home-made fries and sauces, halal, at small prices.

It’s the place to eat in peace, alone with your thoughts, a little away from the bustle of La Plaine. Open Tuesday to Sunday, lunch and dinner.

C3The Noailles institutionTunisia

It’s excellent and generously filled, at small prices. After a Koujina sandwich, the snack quest can call it a day.

C5The detour to ChileChile

San Guchito

Chilean sandwiches · home-made · brand new

Head down towards the Canebière, to Rue Mazagran, to find the only Chilean sandwich shop around. Everything is home-made from local produce: the round bread, the meat braised for hours, the pickles in jars on the counter.

The star is the mechada con queso: pulled beef and melted cheese running everywhere. There is also the milanesa, completos (the Chilean hot dog), empanadas, and a vegetarian sandwich that changes with the seasons. Some evenings, there is Latin American music.

C6The chef’s sandwichMarseille

Razzia

Sandwiches · specialty coffee · rue Fontange

At the top of Rue Fontange, a little yellow-and-blue café: strong espressos in the morning, and at lunch knockout sandwiches, in buns from the bakery next door.

The fillings change often, but there is nearly always a meat one, like the bœufato tonnato (grilled beef, tonnato sauce, capers), and a veggie one, with charred endives and gorgonzola. Home-made pastries to finish.

D1The enthusiastsAsturias

La Nena

Asturian tapas · cider · terrace

On Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont, in a former hair salon, an Asturian tapas bar that opened in 2023. Behind the counter, women who love what they do and give everything to the quality of every plate: it’s impressive.

The cider is served the Asturian way, poured from a height. Croquetas, tortilla, pulpo and an award-winning cachopo, to share on the terrace under the plane tree. Tuesday to Saturday, from 6pm.

D3Good for the heartPalestine

Palestine de Gaza

Mezze · vegetarian · library

On Rue Curiol, just above La Plaine. It’s new, it’s good, and it warms the heart. Vegetarian cooking from Gaza: big mezze plates, falafel, hummus, filled sandwiches.

It also houses a free-access Palestinian library, and hosts talks, workshops and Arabic classes. Tuesday to Sunday, 11:30am to 10pm.

Frequently asked questions

Where to grab a quick bite in Cours Julien?

On Rue Pastoret, within fifty metres: bánh mì at Bánh Mì 24, panzerotti at Panpan Zerotti and filled doughnuts at Bibou Beignets. On Rue des Bergers, the empanadas at Rincón Argentino; on Rue Saint-Michel, the durums at Durum.

Where to eat late at night in La Plaine?

Le Paki, on Place Jean-Jaurès, serves its Pakistani food late, every day. Panpan Zerotti, on Rue Pastoret, and Un Mexicain à Marseille, on Place Paul-Cézanne, serve until 11pm.

Where to find a quick vegetarian bite in Cours Julien?

Restaurant Palestine de Gaza, on Rue Curiol, has an entirely vegetarian menu. Le Petit Diable always has a veggie taco, and Rincón Argentino does spinach, humita or fugazzeta empanadas.

Where to eat tacos in Cours Julien?

Three spots: Tacos Tacos Tacos, on Place Notre-Dame-du-Mont, a counter with good wine; Le Petit Diable, on Rue Crudère, with its home-made tortillas; Un Mexicain à Marseille, on Place Paul-Cézanne, for big appetites.
